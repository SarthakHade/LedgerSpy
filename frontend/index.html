What is LedgerSpy?

LedgerSpy is a forensic audit tool for company ledgers, intended for internal auditors, fraud investigators and compliance teams. It is being rebuilt from a hackathon prototype. This version runs entirely in your browser: the CSV you load is processed on this machine and is not uploaded anywhere.

What this version actually does

📂
CSV Ledger Tools — working
Import a CSV, search, filter, add rows and export the updated ledger.
🧮
Heuristic Risk Preview — working
Hand-set rules and weights score each row 0–100 with an exact points breakdown. Not a trained model; not calibrated.
📊
Leading-Digit Profile — descriptive
Observed vs Benford-expected first digits. No statistical test yet.
🧹
Data Readiness — preliminary
Checks empty/non-numeric amounts, empty vendors and column counts.
🕸️
Graph, Bank Recon, Benchmarks — not yet
Entity resolution, relationship graph, bank reconciliation, Monte Carlo and benchmarking are on the roadmap and show "Not available" until built.
🔒
Local processing
No external fonts, scripts or API calls are loaded by this page.

The scenario LedgerSpy is built for

📌 From the original problem statement (fictional scenario)

Piyush, a young Chartered Accountant, starts a high-stakes audit with a warning from the senior partner: one data leak could cost the firm its licence. With Wi-Fi disabled, he runs LedgerSpy on his laptop against the client's ledger while his teammates scroll through thousands of Excel rows — and uncovers a circular trading network of ghost vendors that had been draining funds for three years.

That scenario is the target. This page is an early step towards it; the roadmap in docs/roadmap.md tracks what is real and what is still to be built.

Monte Carlo Going Concern Stress Test Cash-Flow Simulation

Not available yet. A going-concern simulation needs the company's cash-flow history (at least ~24 months of inflows/outflows and an opening cash balance), which a transaction ledger alone does not provide. When supplied (roadmap P18), LedgerSpy will run thousands of simulated 12-month futures and show the survival probability with percentile bands. No projection is shown until then.
